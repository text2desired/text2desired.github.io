From Words to Desired Images

Language-specified goal views for visual servoing in Gaussian Splatting scenes

Mustapha Boughdaoui  ·  Youssef Alj

International Artificial Intelligence Center of Morocco – Ai movement, Mohammed VI Polytechnic University

Paper (coming soon)Code (coming soon)

Abstract

Visual servoing needs a desired image, which is normally captured or chosen by an operator. We generate it from a text query in a scene reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting. The queried object is grounded with an open-vocabulary detector and a promptable segmenter, lifted to 3D with the rendered depth, and summarised by its centroid, extent and dominant plane. A geometric viewpoint selection renders candidate views and scores their visibility, render reliability, a frontality or broadside prior and whether the whole object fits in the image with the camera in free space; the render at the chosen pose is the desired image. On the 67 labelled queries of LERF-OVS, our desired images are good views (object visible, whole, centred, well sized) more often than those of CLIP render-and-score and CapFrame, as often as the training photograph in which the object was detected but better centred, and always level. A photometric visual servo reaches them more often than the images of every baseline from small offsets. Grounding, not the viewpoint, is the main source of failure.

Each dashboard follows one query. The camera starts at a training photo and is driven to the selected viewpoint by a six-feature servo (object centre, viewing direction, distance and roll). Viewpoint selection

Viewpoint selection for “piano”: 64 of the 74 candidates are far from every training photo, where the 3DGS render is unreliable, and are rejected by the coverage term; the 9 others are shown from lowest to highest score, then the selected view.

Downstream use: reaching it with photometric visual servoing

The method ends with the desired image and its goal pose; any image-based servo can take it as a target. We use a photometric servo, which sees only images, to test it: it starts 0.2 d* and 10° away from the goal and converges to it.

A ground robot in synthetic houses

On InteriorGS houses (Habitat-GS), the camera sits on a ground robot: 1.25 m high, level, on the navigable floor. The viewpoint selection keeps only floor candidates the robot can reach. Each dashboard follows one query: the robot starts at the survey view where the object was detected, follows an A* path on the navigable grid to the selected floor point, turns towards the goal heading, then a 3-DOF photometric servo (forward, lateral, yaw) takes it to the desired image. These videos illustrate the pipeline; the evaluation is in the supplementary material of the paper. Bottom: distance and heading to the goal, photometric error and robot velocities; the shaded part is the servo.
